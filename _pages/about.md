I am a climate scientist with a passion for understanding how Earth's climate system works and how it is changing. I am currently a Postdoctoral Research Associate at the University at Albany, SUNY, with my research based at the National Center for Atmospheric Research (NCAR), Research Applications Laboratory (RAL).

I am originally from Kerala, India. Growing up surrounded by the rhythms of the monsoon sparked an early curiosity about weather and climate, eventually leading me to pursue a career in atmospheric science. I completed my Bachelor's and Master's degrees at the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Kolkata, followed by a Ph.D. in Geosciences at the University of Arizona.

During my doctoral research, I investigated the processes that control hydroclimate variability across South America using high-resolution Earth system models. My work focused on understanding atmospheric moisture transport, land-atmosphere interactions, and the mechanisms that shape regional precipitation across timescales ranging from past climates to future climate change. I also developed and analyzed simulations using water-tracing techniques to identify the sources and pathways of atmospheric moisture.

Today, my research combines climate modeling, observational and reanalysis datasets, and large-scale data analysis to better understand the processes that drive weather and climate.
