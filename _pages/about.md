Hi,

I am a researcher with a passion for understanding how our climate system works and how it is changing. I am currently a Postdoctoral Research Associate at the University at Albany, SUNY, with my research based at the National Center for Atmospheric Research (NCAR), Research Applications Laboratory (RAL).

I am originally from Kerala, India. Growing up surrounded by the rhythms of the monsoon sparked an early curiosity about weather and climate, eventually leading me to pursue a career in climate science. I completed my Bachelor's and Master's degrees at the Indian Institute of Science Education and Research (IISER) Kolkata, followed by a Ph.D. in Geosciences at the University of Arizona.

During my doctoral research, I investigated the processes that control hydroclimate variability across South America using the Community Earth System Model (CESM), a climate model that simulates interactions among the atmosphere, land, ocean, and other parts of the Earth system. My work focused on understanding how moisture moves through the atmosphere and the mechanisms that shape regional precipitation variability across timescales ranging from past climates to the future. I also ran and analyzed simulations using water-tracing-enabled CESM to better understand tropical South American rainfall.

Today, my research combines climate modeling, observations, reanalysis data, and large-scale data analysis to better understand the processes that drive weather and climate. In my current research, I use the Unified Forecast System Single Column Model (UFS SCM), a focused version of a weather and climate model that examines the atmosphere above a specific location, to improve our understanding of atmospheric processes and how they influence weather and climate.
