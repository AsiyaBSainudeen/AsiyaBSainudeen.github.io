This NOAA S2S Program-funded project improves NOAA's Unified Forecast System Single Column Model (UFS SCM) by adding stable water isotopes, which act like natural fingerprints that help reveal where water comes from and how it moves through the atmosphere.

I am developing a separate, diagnostic water cycle within the UFS SCM that follows water as it evaporates, moves through the atmosphere, forms clouds, falls as precipitation, and returns to the surface. This additional water cycle does not change the model's existing weather predictions; it provides extra information for understanding rainfall and other water-cycle processes.

The model will also track moisture from different regions and sources, helping us understand the pathways that lead to heavy rainfall and other important weather events. I will compare these isotope-based simulations with observations to evaluate the model and better understand precipitation and hydrological processes.
